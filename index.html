MISSION GUIDE · LIGHT PATH · MEASUREMENT

How Ariel turns starlight into a spectrum

A telescope does not photograph an exoplanet’s atmosphere directly. Ariel measures the tiny wavelength-dependent change in starlight as a planet crosses its star.

Instrument explainer
FROM PHOTONS TO ATMOSPHERIC CLUES

Follow one beam of light through Ariel.

Collect infrared starlight, split it by wavelength, and compare spectra taken during and outside transit. The atmosphere leaves its signature as small changes in transit depth.

1.1 × 0.7 mSize of Ariel’s primary mirror · ESA mission specification
STEP 1 OF 4

A planet crosses its star

During transit, a small fraction of the starlight passes through the planet’s atmosphere. Molecules absorb particular wavelengths, subtly changing the transmitted spectrum.

THE STORY IN FIVE STEPS

From a passing planet to an atmospheric clue

The mission observes the system; the analysis turns calibrated detector data into a wavelength-by-wavelength transit measurement.

1

Planet crosses its star

The star dims slightly. Some starlight filters through the planet’s atmosphere on its way to the telescope.

2

Mirrors gather the light

Ariel’s primary mirror collects the signal; relay mirrors focus and direct it toward the instruments.

3

Light reaches parallel channels

FGS supports guidance; AIRS splits infrared light into two spectral channels. These are parallel branches.

4

Calibrate and extract

Detector corrections and spectral extraction produce time-series measurements for wavelength bins.

5

Compare transit depths

Depth as a function of wavelength forms a transmission spectrum; atmospheric features may be studied from its shape.

What “spectrum” means here

A spectrum is a set of measurements across wavelength, not a direct photograph of the atmosphere. Interpreting molecular abundances requires further modeling and evidence.

How to read the sketch

The bars are a conceptual illustration of wavelength-dependent transit depth. They are not a saved LARP result and are not molecular detections.

How to use this observatory

1 · Browse
Search any planet or star ID. Filter by saved evidence, fold, or holdout status. The dossier shows catalog metadata for every ID and saved visit details only where available.

2 · Inspect the benchmark
Open Spectrum Lab for the three saved curves, or Transit Theater for the measured extracted series and target geometry. Hover on charts for exact values.

3 · Read the status
Gold labels mean archived outputs; green labels mean catalog data. A missing artifact means “not processed in this snapshot,” not a failed planet.

4 · Keep the science honest
All 3D scenes are schematic. This app is a browser presentation of saved files; it does not rerun extraction, training, calibration, or retrieval.

LARP Research Observatory

This offline JavaScript app browses local catalog and archived analysis outputs. It does not run the pipeline.

  • Use the top search for IDs; select a row or scatter point to change planets.
  • “Archived output” marks saved notebooks/contracts that were not regenerated from edited sources.
  • Only one target has full spectrum and retrieval artifacts; other catalog rows show only available evidence.
  • Population OOF metrics from historical surrogate outputs are intentionally excluded.
